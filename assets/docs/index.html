v3.0.0 · Checked against the real store

New in 3.0

This release was built by opening real PlayStation Store pages and testing the extension against them. Here is what changed, in plain words.

PlayStation price history, built in your browser

The Price Insight card now shows the lowest PlayStation price you have seen, today's price and the sale: what it was, the percent off, when it ends and how long it has left (“Offer ends Nov 21 (in 14 days)”). It adds the PlayStation Plus price when the page shows one, the store's own “lowest price in the last 30 days”, a trend, and how far the price is from the cheapest PC price.

  • Lowest PS price you've seen
  • Today's price
  • Sale and time left
  • PS Plus price
  • Store's 30-day low
  • Trend
  • Gap to the cheapest PC price

It is built only from prices your browser recorded, and says “not enough history yet” until there are two different days. History keeps the latest 400 days with a recorded price and is included in the JSON backup. Prices are also recorded from search results (for games you have already looked at or wishlisted) and, once a day, from the saved page of each wishlisted game: at most 20 pages, no cookies, store.playstation.com only. Turning off Price insight in Settings stops both.

Check this page

A button in the toolbar popup that runs every lookup the extension depends on in the current store tab and shows which ones work, which work through a fallback and which are not found.

If a store redesign breaks one lookup, only that card is switched off on that page and the Diagnostics log says why, instead of the whole page failing.

Hebrew and Arabic read right to left

On the cards, the popup, the settings page and the welcome page. That includes the card's collapse arrow, which used to point the wrong way, and the prices in the search suggestions and the cross-platform note, which used to come out scrambled.

See the Hebrew screenshot

A new look: Midnight Glass

The cards on store pages, the toolbar popup, the settings page, the welcome page and the changelog now share one design: glass surfaces, a bigger headline number for the lowest price and one set of line icons in place of most emoji. Dark, light and high contrast are on the cards, the popup, the settings page and the changelog; the welcome page follows your system's light or dark setting.

Three small buttons in the popup header switch between Auto, Dark and Light. Cards already open on a store tab change at once, and Auto follows your system. High contrast stays in Settings.

  • Dark
  • Light
  • High contrast

Cards stay on the page

The store re-renders its page a few seconds after it loads, which used to remove the cards. They are now put back (up to three times per page) without starting the page over: the first-run tour keeps its step and the price is not recorded twice.

PSN connect works in Chrome

Connecting PSN failed in Chrome every time; it is fixed. It stays opt-in, and everything else works without it. The fix adds one permission, declarativeNetRequestWithHostAccess, which Chrome shows no warning for and which the extension uses for nothing else.

Checked on one real account.

Report a problem

A button in the popup and in Settings → Diagnostics copies the version, the browser and the diagnostics log (already free of tokens) to the clipboard, then opens the GitHub bug-report form, where you paste it.

Nothing is put in the address, and the extension sends nothing itself.

The Rubik font is bundled

Rubik (SIL Open Font License) ships inside the extension, about 110 KB covering Latin, Hebrew, Cyrillic and Arabic, so the look no longer depends on the fonts installed on your computer. It is read from the extension itself and never downloaded. This site uses the same font files, served from the site itself.

Checked against real store pages

The automated tests now run the real content script against saved real store pages (product pages in two regions and a search grid). That found bugs in v2.7.1 that the old test pages could not show, among them a wrong PlayStation price that could be recorded on pages with an edition list, the “Hide add-ons”, “Hide DLC” and “Hide games I already own” filters missing or hiding the wrong games, and blank gaps left in the search grid by hidden results. They are fixed.

What was checked, exactly: the PSN sign-in on one real account, and the injected cards by tests against the real page structure and by eye in Chrome with the extension loaded.

Features

Everything the PS Store should already do

All core features work without sign-in. PSN integration is opt-in.

Price insight, real facts only

The real lowest PC price ever (with its date), today's cheapest and a “near the all-time low” flag, plus a trend drawn from prices your own browser recorded and a one-click link to PSPrices for the full PlayStation price history. Never an invented chart.

Review scores at a glance

Metacritic (PC) and Steam user-rating badges next to the game title, colour-coded green, yellow or red. Shown only when a real score exists, never a guess.

Exact cross-platform comparison

The same game's prices on Steam, Epic, GOG, Humble and more, matched to the exact game (never a DLC, map or sequel) and converted to your currency.

Edition transparency

A price for a different bundle or edition (Deluxe, Ultimate, GOTY…) than the page you are viewing is flagged, never blended in silently.

Live search suggestions

Type in the store's own search box and see a dropdown with a thumbnail and the cheapest PC price for each match, before you even hit enter.

Trophy info

Platinum availability, difficulty (1–10), estimated hours, a one-glance completionist effort score and price per hour for 75 popular titles (a community-maintained database), plus your own live trophy progress when PSN is connected.

Wishlist alerts

Track games, set a price or “% off” target, add notes, and get a desktop notification (or one weekly digest) when the cheapest PC price reaches it. Share the list as a read-only link: nothing is uploaded.

Library and wishlist stats

Games owned, wishlist size, total wishlist value and deals ready to buy: one dashboard, computed from data on your device.

Toolbar deal badge

The extension icon shows a count of the wishlist deals at or below your target price.

18 currencies

EUR, GBP, ILS, JPY, BRL, KRW, MXN, INR and more, set from your store region, with daily rates from Frankfurter (European Central Bank data).

Smart filters

Hide add-ons, currency packs, DLC and games you already own. It works with infinite scroll.

Dark, light and high contrast

Auto matches your system, or choose Dark or Light in the popup; high contrast is in Settings. Reduced motion and visible keyboard focus are respected.

Your page, your cards

Reorder, hide or collapse the product-page cards, share a deal as an image, and take a first-run tour that you can skip.

Privacy first

No analytics, no tracking, no remote code. What the extension remembers lives in your browser's extension storage, and one click erases it.

Privacy

Private by design

No analytics, no tracking, no data sharing, and no servers of our own.

  • No analytics, no trackingNo Google Analytics, no telemetry, no tracking service of any kind.
  • No remote codeThe extension does not use any remotely hosted code, and its fonts are packaged with it.
  • Your data stays on your deviceSettings, wishlist, owned games, recorded prices and logs are kept in your browser's extension storage, not on a server of ours.
  • One click erases it“Erase all my data” (popup and Settings) disconnects PSN and deletes the wishlist, owned games, caches, recorded prices and logs. Your preference settings are kept.
  • Pictures are loaded by your browserThe small thumbnails in live search (mostly from Steam's image server) and, if you connect PSN, your avatar are plain image loads made by the browser itself, so those servers see what any image request shows, such as your IP address. Turn off Live Search Suggestions to stop the thumbnails.

The only servers it talks to

Five documented hosts. The network layer refuses any other, a test fails if this list and the privacy texts disagree, and Settings shows a log of every request (host, path, status and time, never the query, headers or data).

  • www.cheapshark.com

    Exact-game lookup, cross-platform prices, the lowest-ever PC price, Metacritic and Steam scores, and live search suggestions (text only).

  • api.frankfurter.dev

    Daily exchange rates. Only currency codes are sent.

  • store.playstation.com

    The store itself. Once a day the saved page of each wishlisted game is read (at most 20, no cookies) to record its price.

  • ca.account.sony.comOptional

    PSN sign-in, only if you connect PSN.

  • m.np.playstation.comOptional

    PSN library and trophies, only if you connect PSN.

Developer install

  1. Download the zip from the latest GitHub release.
  2. Unzip it.
  3. Open chrome://extensions in Chrome.
  4. Turn on Developer mode (top right).
  5. Click Load unpacked and choose the unzipped folder (the one that contains manifest.json).
  6. Visit store.playstation.com and open a game.
